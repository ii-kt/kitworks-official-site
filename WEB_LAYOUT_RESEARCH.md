# KiT Works / Business Website Layout Research

Date: 2026-08-10

This implementation follows a direct review of 32 current official business websites. The review compared first-view hierarchy, content order, text measure, evidence, calls to action, responsive collapse, and motion roles. Directory thumbnails and copied templates were not treated as evidence.

## Repeated layout patterns

- The first view states what the business changes, for whom, in one short claim. A long manifesto or an abstract diagram does not replace that definition.
- Trust or evidence follows immediately: selected work, clients, an outcome, a testimonial, or verifiable operating facts.
- Work is shown before services, or with equal visual weight. Services alone do not prove capability.
- Body copy is deliberately narrow. For Japanese, the target is roughly 28–40 characters per line, normally no more than 600–660 px at 16–18 px with a 1.7–1.9 line height.
- Desktop layouts commonly use an asymmetric 12-column grid. The main claim occupies about seven columns and factual supporting information about five.
- Important sections use different scale rather than a uniform card grid. Hairlines, whitespace, and type hierarchy carry the layout.
- Mobile preserves the semantic order, collapses to one column, uses 16–24 px side padding, and removes nonessential horizontal or drag-only behavior.
- Motion has a job: one strong entrance, brief section reveals, restrained hover/press feedback, and at most one ambient loop per view.
- A service site converges on one next action. KiT Works keeps that action at the entrance or within the selected Project document, without adding cross-document navigation.

## Applied KiT Works rules

1. Keep the old-Windows shell, clicked-origin opening, decode, Close-only contract, and background ghost.
2. Use a contemporary Japanese editorial/business layout inside the shell.
3. Each destination starts with a direct page-specific proposition and a short explanation.
4. Abstract diagrams cannot occupy the supporting five columns unless they prove the page's subject. Welcome and About therefore use service and business-profile facts instead.
5. Welcome reads: proposition → services at a glance → three operating facts → small operator note.
6. Capabilities uses factual full-width service rows; Process, Approach, FAQ, Project, and Availability use purpose-specific split layouts.
7. No fabricated clients, metrics, testimonials, or projects are introduced. Portfolio communicates its release state and future case-study standard honestly.
8. On mobile, every document becomes one column and remains scrollable inside the viewer.
